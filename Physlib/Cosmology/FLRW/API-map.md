# FLRW cosmology

## Abstract (Alpha)

A Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker spacetime models a universe that is homogeneous and
isotropic. Its geometry is fixed by a scale factor, a function of cosmic time on the time
manifold, together with the curvature of the spatial slices, which is spherical, flat or saddle.
The expansion rate is the Hubble parameter, its change is measured by the deceleration parameter,
and the dynamics is governed by the Friedmann equations, which tie the scale factor to the
energy density and pressure of the cosmic fluid, to the spatial curvature and to the cosmological
constant. Around these sit the continuity equation, barotropic equations of state with their
density scaling laws, the cosmological constant as a fluid, exact solutions, the density
parameters and their closure relation, the reduced Hubble function of the standard cosmological
model, the age of the universe, the energy conditions of the cosmic fluid with the acceleration
criterion, and eternal expansion together with the Big-Bang bound on the interval of cosmic time.

## Content (Alpha)

### Geometry and the FLRW model

The constant-time slices have one of three spatial geometries, spherical, flat or saddle, with
metric profile giving the transverse radius k sin(r/k), r, or k sinh(r/k) in the three
cases. [1] The flat geometry is the large-radius limit of the curved ones: the profiles of the
spherical and saddle geometries tend to the flat profile as the curvature radius tends to
infinity. [2]

An FLRW model is a scale factor, positive and twice differentiable on an open interval of cosmic
time {1}, together with a spatial geometry. [3] The spatial geometry supplies the curvature
parameter of the Friedmann equations, positive for the spherical geometry, zero for the flat one
and negative for the saddle one. [3] (For the Robertson–Walker metric see Weinberg {2} and
Baumann {3}.)

### The Friedmann equations

The first-order Friedmann equation (∂ₜ a / a)² = (8πG/3) ρ − k c² / a² + Λ c² / 3 relates a
scale factor and an energy density, with the curvature parameter explicit; the second-order
Friedmann equation ∂ₜ ∂ₜ a / a = −(4πG/3) (ρ + 3 p / c²) + Λ c² / 3 involves a pressure as well
and has no curvature term. The cosmological constant, Newton's constant and the speed of light
appear explicitly in both. [8]

A cosmic fluid is an energy density and a pressure, each a function of cosmic time; the empty
fluid has zero density and pressure. [4] A model filled with a fluid solves the Friedmann equations
when both hold on the interval of the model, the curvature parameter being read off the spatial
geometry. [4] Every such solution satisfies the continuity equation [5] and the closure relation
of the density parameters. [6] The de Sitter model at all times, and the Milne model after its
Big Bang, each filled with the empty fluid, solve the Friedmann equations. [17][7] The flat
power-law models, filled with their fluid, solve the Friedmann equations on t > 0 for the matching
equation of state. [25] The radiation-dominated and Einstein–de Sitter models are the cases n = 1/2
and n = 2/3, and after their Big Bang they too solve the Friedmann equations. [26][27]

### Hubble and deceleration parameters

The Hubble parameter H = ∂ₜ a / a is a function of the scale factor and cosmic time, and is
nonzero wherever the scale factor and its derivative both are. [9] The deceleration parameter is
q = −(∂ₜ ∂ₜ a) a / (∂ₜ a)². The derivative ∂ₜ H obeys the quotient-rule formula, q equals
−(1 + (∂ₜ H) / H²) and ∂ₜ H equals −H² (1 + q), and ∂ₜ H < 0 holds exactly when q > −1, both
pointwise and in existential form. [10]

### Matter content

The continuity equation ∂ₜ ρ + 3 H (ρ + P/c²) = 0 of the cosmic fluid holds at an instant
whenever the first-order Friedmann equation holds throughout a neighbourhood of that instant
together with the second-order equation; conversely, the second-order equation follows from the
first-order equation and the continuity equation wherever the scale factor is changing. [11]
(See Dodelson and Schmidt {4} for the smooth, expanding universe.)

Under the barotropic equation of state P = w ρ c², the continuity equation implies the density
scaling law ρ t = ρ t₀ (a t / a t₀)^(−3(1+w)), and conversely; dust, radiation and vacuum energy
are special cases. [12] The cosmological constant is itself a fluid, with density
ρ_Λ = Λ c² / (8πG) and pressure p_Λ = −ρ_Λ c², and folding it into the cosmic fluid gives the
fluid with density ρ + ρ_Λ and pressure p + p_Λ. [18] Each Friedmann equation with a cosmological
constant is equivalent to the same equation without one for the combined fluid, and that
pressure is the w = −1 barotropic pressure of that density. [13] Hence a solution of the Friedmann
equations with Λ is a solution without Λ for the combined fluid. [19]

### Density parameters, the standard model and the age

The critical density is ρ_cr = 3 H² / (8πG), and the density parameters are Ω = ρ / ρ_cr,
Ω_K = −k c² / (H² a²) and Ω_Λ = Λ c² / (3 H²); the first-order Friedmann equation is the closure
relation Ω + Ω_Λ + Ω_K = 1. [14]

The standard cosmological model has reduced Hubble function
E(x)² = Ω_Λ + Ω_m x⁻³ + Ω_r x⁻⁴ + Ω_K x⁻², with H = H₀ E(a / a₀) for matter and radiation obeying
their scaling laws, and with radiation–matter and matter–Λ equality scale factors. [15] The age
of the universe is t₀ = H₀⁻¹ ∫₀¹ dx / (x E(x)). [16]

### Energy conditions and acceleration

The null, weak, strong and dominant energy conditions of the cosmic fluid are defined, with the
implications between them: the dominant condition implies the weak one, and the weak and strong
conditions each imply the null one. [20] On the interval of a solution of the Friedmann equations
the universe accelerates, 0 < ∂ₜ ∂ₜ a, exactly when ρ + 3 p / c² < 0, without and with Λ; and the
deceleration parameter is q = ½ Σ_i Ω_i (1 + 3 w_i) in terms of the density parameters, with its
ΛCDM special case. [21]

### Eternal expansion and the Big Bang

An FLRW model that decelerates on its interval and is expanding at t₀ lies below its tangent line
at t₀, so it cannot be defined earlier than t₀ − 1 / H(t₀) and its interval is bounded below. [22]
On the interval of a solution, for a spatial geometry of non-positive curvature, 0 ≤ Λ and positive
density, the expansion never stops; with 0 ≤ ρ, 0 ≤ p and Λ = 0 the solution decelerates, so it
has a Big Bang in the finite past. [23] The de Sitter model, by contrast, is defined at all times.
[24]
